Why TCP?

TCP is a layer of abstraction over the Internet protocol itself. It allows for data to be sent between two nodes in a secure and ordered fashion.

How TCP?

The Three-Way Handshake

A TCP connection is initiated through a handshake, consisting of a SYN (synchronize) from the client, a SYN-ACK (synchronize-acknowledge) from the server, and an ACK (acknowledge) from the client.

Data Transmission

TCP separates parts of a block of data and splits it into segments. Each of these segments gets a unique sequence number, so the receiver can put the pieces back in the right order upon receiving the data. Upon transmission of a segment, the sender notes down the time the packet was originally transmitted. This will become important later.

As the receiver parses each segment, it sends an ACK message back to the sender to... acknowledge the retrieval. If this message is not received by the sender after the time surpasses the original transmission time + a configurable timeout, the sender transmits the original message again and repeats the process until it receives an ACK or times out.

Connection Termination

A TCP connection is terminated via a FIN (Finish) from the client, an ACK from the server, a FIN from the server, and an ACK from the client, in that order.
